**Sep 25, 2026.** Congratulations to [Dr. Catherine Talbot](https://www.fit.edu/faculty-profiles/t/talbot-catherine-f/), an associate professor in Florida Tech's School of Psychology, on sharing the **2026 Ig Nobel Prize in biomechanics** with Matilda Brindle and Stuart West of the University of Oxford. The [official announcement from Improbable Research](https://improbable.com/ig/winners/) recognizes the team's work on defining kissing across species.

<!--more-->

Their study, ["A comparative approach to the evolution of kissing"](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2025.106788), published in *Evolution and Human Behavior*, investigates the evolutionary history of kissing. By establishing a definition that can be applied across species, the researchers examined how this familiar behavior fits into the broader story of animal social interactions.

The [organizers of the Ig Nobel Prizes](https://improbable.com/ig/winners/) describe the awards as recognizing achievements that "first make people LAUGH then make them THINK." The prizes offer a playful setting for sharing research with audiences beyond a scientist's own field.

As fellow members of the Florida Tech community, we are proud to celebrate this recognition of Dr. Talbot and her co-authors. Their achievement is a welcome reminder of the value of curiosity and the breadth of research taking place across our university.

Read more in [Florida Tech's coverage of the award](https://news.fit.edu/psychology_behavior_analysis/talbot-shares-ig-nobel-prize-for-research-into-biomechanics-evolution-of-kissing/).

Congratulations to Dr. Talbot and the entire team!
